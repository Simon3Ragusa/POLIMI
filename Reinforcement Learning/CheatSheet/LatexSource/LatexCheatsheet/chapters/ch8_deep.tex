\section{Advanced Deep Reinforcement Learning}

\subsection{Ensuring Stability: TRPO and PPO}
Standard PG methods can suffer from large, destructive policy updates. If the parameters $\theta$ change too much, the data collected by the old policy becomes useless, and learning can collapse. To solve this, we use \textbf{Trust Region} methods.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TRPO (Trust Region Policy Optimization):} It enforces a constraint on the KL-divergence between the old and new policy. While theoretically sound, it is mathematically complex and computationally expensive.
    \item \textbf{PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization):} PPO achieves a similar goal but much more simply. It uses a \textit{clipped objective function} that prevents the policy from changing more than a certain percentage ($1 \pm \epsilon$) from the old policy. This makes PPO one of the most popular and robust algorithms in Deep RL today.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Continuous Control: DDPG and TD3}
For environments with continuous actions (like robot arm control), we cannot use DQN. Instead, we use \textbf{DDPG (Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient)}. DDPG is an actor-critic method where the actor learns a \textit{deterministic} policy $\mu(s)$. Like DQN, it uses experience replay and target networks to ensure stability.

However, DDPG is notoriously sensitive to hyperparameters and often overestimates Q-values. \textbf{TD3 (Twin Delayed DDPG)} fixes these issues with three specific tricks: using the \textit{minimum} of two critics (clipped double-Q), \textit{smoothing} the target policy with noise, and \textit{delaying} the actor updates to allow the critic to stabilize first.

\subsection{Maximum Entropy RL: Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)}
A different philosophy is taken by \textbf{Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)}. Instead of just maximizing the return, SAC maximizes the return \textbf{plus the entropy} of the policy.
\[ J(\theta) = \sum_t \mathbb{E} [ R_t + \alpha H(\pi(\cdot|S_t)) ] \]
This encourages the agent to be as random as possible while still getting high rewards. The result is an algorithm that explores much more effectively, is robust to noise, and is highly stable compared to DDPG.

\subsection{The Role of Importance Sampling}
When we learn about one policy (the target $\pi$) using data generated by another (the behavior $\beta$), we are doing \textbf{Off-policy learning}. To correct for the discrepancy between these two distributions, we use \textbf{Importance Sampling}. By multiplying our updates by the ratio $\rho_t = \frac{\pi(A_t|S_t)}{\beta(A_t|S_t)}$, we ensure that our estimates remain unbiased. This technique is fundamental to PPO, PER, and many other modern algorithms.
